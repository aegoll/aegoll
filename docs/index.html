Autonomous payment should not mean unrestricted payment

Tesoro sits between an agent and every dollar it spends — the tokens it burns thinking and the money it pays out — and decides, before the payment, whether it should happen. Ten deterministic engines, no model in the decision path, and an append-only record of every decision and the control that made it. 

# the whole thing
pip install tesoro
tesoro init
tesoro decide --amount 2.50 --vendor acme --resource /market/snapshot

What is an Autonomous Economic Governance Layer?

An AEGL is any layer that decides whether an autonomous agent may spend, before it spends. That is the whole category definition, and the word doing the work is before.

It matters because an agent is not a person with a card. It acts thousands of times an hour, it has no intuition about money, and it cannot be asked mid-run whether a purchase seems sensible. Every traditional control assumes a human somewhere in the loop — a monthly statement, an approval workflow, a fraud alert after the fact. None of those work at machine speed, and all of them are reports rather than controls.

Four kinds of AEGL, and what each can express

Not every layer that says no is the same thing. These are ordered by what they can express, and each row can do everything above it:

TypeDecides onCatchesCannot catch
1 · Per-payment cap one amount, in isolation a single payment that is too large ten payments that are each fine
2 · Cumulative envelope amount + history a budget exhausted over a day, a month, a window a perfectly affordable purchase of the wrong thing
3 · Governed budget tesoro amount, history, counterparty, purpose, identity drift from what the agent was sent to do; an untrusted counterparty; a delegate exceeding its parent the shape of a sequence
4 · Behavioural not built all of the above + sequence shape structuring — forty payments below every limit —

Type 1 is now table stakes. The x402 SDKs ship a per-payment cap, and so do several others. If a spend cap is all you need, you may already have one.

Tesoro is type 3, and the gap between 2 and 3 is the interesting one: “within budget” and “doing the job” are different questions, and a budget cannot answer the second.

Why a cap on one payment is not a budget

Forty payments of one cent pass every per-payment cap ever written. So do five identical purchases when only the sixth exhausts the day. Here is the same one-cent purchase, six times, against a ten dollar per-payment ceiling that none of them comes near: 

purchase 1: $0.01  APPROVE
purchase 2: $0.01  APPROVE
purchase 3: $0.01  APPROVE
purchase 4: $0.01  APPROVE
purchase 5: $0.01  APPROVE
purchase 6: $0.01  REVIEW  ← treasury / binding=daily

The refused payment is byte-for-byte identical to the five that were approved. A cap with no memory cannot express that, and no amount of tightening a per-payment ceiling produces it — tightening only makes legitimate use harder.

The three things Tesoro produces

A decision

One of four verdicts, made before money moves. Deterministic integer arithmetic — the same inputs always give the same answer.

An attribution

Which control decided, not just what was decided. The field you actually need at 2am, and the one a single cap cannot produce.

Evidence

An append-only, hash-chained record. Editing, middle-deletion and reordering are all detected.

The four verdicts, ordered by severity

APPROVE < REVIEW < ESCALATE < REJECT

The ordering is normative, and the middle pair is the one that invites disagreement: REVIEW is pausable — a human can look at it and the agent can wait. ESCALATE is blocking. Controls combine by narrowing only: any control may tighten a verdict another set, and none may widen one. Widening is not refused — it is unreachable, which is a stronger property than a rule nobody enforces.

What Tesoro does not do

Stated here rather than in a footnote, because the rest of this site is only worth reading if this section is honest. Every item is also in the CHANGELOG and the specification's security section.

  • Structuring is undefended. Forty payments of a tenth of a cent, paced apart, breach no value envelope and no rate limit — 0.08% of a daily budget, nothing refused. It ships as a runnable demonstration rather than a paragraph: usecases/aml-structuring. Catching it needs a control that reads the shape of a sequence, and no envelope does.
  • Velocity evasion is undefended. Pacing exactly at a rate limit is unbounded in total: ten per minute permits fourteen thousand a day.
  • Journal truncation is undetectable without an external anchor. Any prefix of a valid chain is itself valid, so an agent that was refused could delete the refusal and verification would still report success. Tesoro prints that caveat next to every VALID rather than shipping a head.json beside the journal — which would look like a fix and defend against nothing, since whoever can truncate the journal can rewrite the file next to it.
  • AML screening is a schema with no engine behind it. No list, no matching, no jurisdiction model. No regulatory compliance is claimed or sought.
  • No independent implementation has been scored. Every conformance case and threat was written by the author of the system under test. That is the largest open question in the project.
  • The CrewAI adapter's hooks are unverified against a real run — documentation only. Its governance behaviour is tested as thoroughly as the others; the hook names are not.

0.x. The API may change before 1.0. This is stated here, on the README, in the CHANGELOG header and at the end of the quickstart, because you are about to point it at a wallet.

The red-team suite's own one-line summary of this layer, kept because it is the fairest available: it resists prose; it did not resist a minus sign, and it does not yet resist patience. The minus sign is fixed and permanently covered by a test vector. Patience is still open.
